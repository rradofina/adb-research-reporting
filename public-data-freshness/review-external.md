## Likely reviewer objections

**“An indicator frontier is not a release calendar.”** Correct. The frontier
is an empirical comparison point inside a pinned WDI response, not a formal
deadline. The paper calls relative lag a review-triage signal.

**“You are excusing old environmental data.”** No. A three-year-old PM2.5 or
forest observation remains three years old. The decomposition only shows that
all observed economies share the same frontier in these baseline indicators;
calendar age remains visible.

**“The headline disappears without environment.”** The broad pass/fail claim
does. That is why the paper reports the 9.2% deletion result and narrows the
conclusion to selected domains with shared production cycles.

**“The cutoff determines the result.”** Substantially. The disagreement share
is 52.6%, 19.5%, and 0.7% at effective cutoffs of two, three, and five years.
The operational recommendation is to expose the rule and underlying ages, not
to declare one universal stale/fresh threshold.

**“WDI is not the original source.”** Correct. The study audits what a WDI-
based dashboard reader sees. Attribution of delay or quality would require
indicator-by-indicator source tracing and formal release metadata.

**“Pacific comparisons are selected by missingness.”** Correct. The paper
leads that diagnostic with coverage: 15.7% missing versus 2.4% elsewhere. It
does not interpret the similar observed-cell disagreement rates as equal data
conditions.

**“Why include so many charts?”** Each figure has a non-decorative role:
coverage, construct, primary count, domain concentration, missingness,
grouped coverage, three sensitivity families, and the final claim gate. The
paper does not add a map or country ranking because neither changes the claim.

## Red-team verdict

The study is defensible as a transparent WDI dashboard audit if every public
surface keeps three distinctions: calendar age versus relative lag, observed
old versus missing, and source-relative review versus formal timeliness. It
would become misleading if framed as an economy score, an excuse for old
data, or proof of national producer delay.
