# Source Patterns

This note summarizes public ADB/ADB Data/ADB ERDI examples used to derive the skill. Use it as a style audit checklist, not as text to copy.

## Observed Examples

- ADB Data Library positions itself as a public data platform with machine-readable, shareable, API-accessible datasets. Its data stories are meant to contextualize and help readers make sense of data. Source: https://data.adb.org/about
- ADB Data Library dataset pages use a highly structured evidence pattern: short dataset description, downloadable resources, metadata table, publisher, license, granularity, access level, temporal coverage, related content, and machine-readable metadata links. Example: https://data.adb.org/dataset/basic-statistics-asia-and-pacific
- ERDI-published data pages identify the publisher as Asian Development Bank / ERDI and present economic and social data as public, licensed datasets. Source: https://data.adb.org/taxonomy/term/105
- Key Indicators explains its own reader path: highlights, SDG data tables, data stories, thematic tables, charts, source notes, availability rules, and economy group definitions. This is a strong pattern for evidence products that combine narrative and tables. Source: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/900716/ki2023.pdf
- Development Asia's "Using Machine Learning on Satellite Images to Map Poverty" starts with SDG/data-disaggregation pressure, explains why conventional poverty data lack granularity, introduces satellite imagery and machine learning, then walks through analysis, results, recommendations, caveats, and resources. Source: https://development.asia/insight/using-machine-learning-satellite-images-map-poverty
- Development Asia's "How Satellite Data Helped Get Food to the Hungry during COVID-19" uses an applied case pattern: crisis context, targeting problem, data integration method, implementation design considerations, field validation, and practical conclusion. Source: https://development.asia/explainer/how-satellite-data-helped-get-food-hungry-during-covid-19
- The Brookings article coauthored by Arturo Martinez Jr. uses a policy-commentary version of the same pattern: high-level data problem, poverty-targeting stakes, conventional data limits, satellite/machine-learning method, result, and why data communication matters. Source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/asias-data-frontier-modeling-poverty-from-space/
- The small-area-estimation guide credits Arturo Martinez Jr. leading publication work and frames ADB statistics work as support for national statistical offices and granular policymaking. Source: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/609476/small-area-estimation-guide-nsos.pdf
- The mapping-poverty supplement credits Arturo Martinez Jr. leading a publication that documents a feasibility study combining traditional methods and innovative sources to improve granularity, cost effectiveness, and high-quality poverty statistics. Source: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/630406/mapping-poverty-ki2020-supplement.pdf

## Reusable Narrative Moves

1. Open with the policy or statistical-capacity need.
2. Name the target user: NSOs, governments, planners, ADB operations, or development partners.
3. State the conventional-data constraint plainly before presenting the new method.
4. Explain the source integration as a complement to official statistics, not as a replacement.
5. Use charts/tables as the spine of the narrative.
6. Add methodological caveats near the result, not only at the end.
7. End with practical use and next improvement.

## Avoid

- Treating AI, satellite imagery, or open data as a complete substitute for official statistics.
- Hiding coverage problems.
- Writing as if the data product itself makes a policy decision.
- Making cross-economy ranking claims without harmonized definitions and caveats.
- Using academic-method density before the reader understands the policy question.
