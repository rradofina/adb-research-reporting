# The annual screen looked stable—but measured the wrong thing

An inherited food-price screen found Lao PDR and Pakistan in the top ranks of
both headline CPI inflation and agricultural raw-material import share. The
intersection survived several top-N choices. Stability made it look publishable.

But neither indicator observes a climate shock or a local food market. Nepal,
where a public market-month price and rainfall object already existed, sits
outside the annual screen's top 10. The ranking could not validate the mechanism
it was being asked to describe.

![The annual qualifier misses the country with the usable market-month object](/programs/food-price-climate-transmission/generated/charts/food-price-macro-market-mismatch.svg)

# The first market story also needed correction

The Nepal sprint compared each coarse-rice price with that market's 2019–2025
median for the same calendar month. In a period with rising price levels, later
observations become “anomalies” even when the relevant research question is
price change.

Replacing that transformation with year-on-year log change reduces spike cells
from 259 to 152 and broad wave months from 25 to 10.

![A change measure removes price-level artifacts](/programs/food-price-climate-transmission/generated/charts/food-price-method-correction.svg)

# The corrected result is smaller and stronger

The panel has 760 market-month observations with comparable year-on-year prices
and one-month-lag rainfall. Under the main thresholds, 152 are price spikes.
Only 17 also follow locally dry rainfall.

![Most corrected spike cells are not locally dry-aligned](/programs/food-price-climate-transmission/generated/charts/food-price-spike-alignment.svg)

Changing the arbitrary thresholds by ±50% produces 81 specifications. Dry
alignment ranges from zero to 48.8% and remains a minority in every one. The
direction is stable; the exact numbers of broad waves and dry clusters are not.

# A broad wave still deserves explanation

From July 2023 through June 2024, several months show price spikes in at least
half of observed markets. September 2023 has spikes in 10 of 12 markets but no
local dryness alignment. That pattern is consistent with a connected or common
driver, but the current data cannot distinguish production, transport, fuel,
trade, exchange rates, policy, or expectations.

![The broad 2023–2024 wave rarely coincides with local dryness](/programs/food-price-climate-transmission/generated/charts/food-price-wave-timeline.svg)

# What a proper transmission study requires

Nepal climate-price transmission is not an empty literature. Baptista, Spray,
and Unsal already use recorded climate events, district-level WFP prices, fixed
effects, and local projections [@baptista2023climateshocks]. Shively and Thapa
show why roads and fuel matter for Nepal market integration
[@shively2017nepalfoodprices].

The next study should update that evidence with newer events, multiple
commodities, crop and sourcing zones, connectivity, and common-driver controls.
Until then, 17 of 152 is a coincidence count—not an estimate of climate's
contribution to food-price inflation.
