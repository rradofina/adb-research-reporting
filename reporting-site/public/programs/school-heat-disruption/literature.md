# Literature and framing

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

## Direct disruption evidence

UNICEF's *Learning Interrupted* provides the public country-level 2024 object
used in the construct validation [@unicef2025learninginterrupted]. It reports
affected students and the major hazard for the largest disruption, while
warning that reporting coverage and estimation vary.

The World Bank's *Choosing Our Future* places climate-related school closures,
learning, adaptation, and education-system response in a wider policy frame
[@worldbank2024choosingfuture]. It does not supply the school-day panel needed
to validate this program's country index.

## Heat and learning

Park, Goodman, Hurwitz, and Smith link hotter school-year exposure to lower
educational performance in US administrative data [@park2020heat]. Their
nonlinear exposure design supports time-aligned heat measurement, not the
inherited annual-national linear ramp.

Wargocki, Porras-Salazar, and Contreras-Espinoza synthesize 18 studies on
classroom temperature and children's performance [@wargocki2019classroom].
The evidence motivates indoor thermal measurement while its concentration in
temperate settings limits direct transfer to developing Asia.

## Evidence spine

The literature supports a mechanism from heat exposure to educational
disruption or performance. It does not support multiplying child population,
pupil-teacher ratio, and historical annual maximum temperature as a validated
country outcome. The next study must align exposure, calendar, population, and
outcome at a shared unit.
