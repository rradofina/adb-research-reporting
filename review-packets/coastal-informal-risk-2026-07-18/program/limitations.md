# Limitations — what the object cannot establish

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

1. **Reporting subset.** The result covers 1,334 centres with reported LECZ
   fields, not every matched centre or every ADB developing economy. The 4,013
   blank blocks are not assumed to be zero.
2. **Urban-centre threshold.** Small island and small-settlement contexts are
   poorly represented. Seven coastal-roster economies have no separable centre.
3. **Fixed footprints.** Historical values are zonal statistics inside a 2025
   urban-centre boundary. The analysis measures change within that footprint,
   not boundary expansion over time.
4. **Elevation is not hazard.** Below 5 or 10 metres does not encode storm
   surge, waves, river flooding, subsidence, relative sea level, return periods,
   or defence standards.
5. **Exposure is not risk or loss.** Risk requires hazard, exposure,
   vulnerability, and adaptation. No expected damage or people-at-risk estimate
   is produced.
6. **Settlement is not informality.** Population and built-up surface do not
   identify tenure, services, dwelling durability, crowding, or legal status.
7. **Centre scale.** Absolute rankings favour large urban centres. The paper
   pairs counts with shares and concentration diagnostics but does not claim a
   vulnerability league table.
8. **Model and source uncertainty.** GHS-POP, GHS-BUILT-S, and SEDAC LECZ are
   modeled geospatial products. This pass varies elevation and time, not their
   full underlying uncertainty distributions.
