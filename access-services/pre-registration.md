## 1. Frozen legacy module — 2026-04-27

The original module pre-registered an eight-DMC OSM/population screen and
tested whether the same four economies remained in its top four under two
country aggregations. That aggregation test passed. It is preserved as
research history, but its access interpretation is superseded because both
aggregations retain the same unvalidated OSM denominator.

Original population: PHL, BGD, PAK, NPL, LKA, KHM, LAO, TLS; 104 ADM1 units.
Original switch: population-weighted mean versus maximum-ADM1 load. Original
common set: BGD, KHM, LAO, PAK.

## 2. Post-hoc denominator-falsification module — formalized 2026-07-18

This module was not pre-registered before the registry deepening. It is
therefore labeled **post-hoc claim reshaping**, not confirmatory evidence.

### Question

Does replacing OSM health-point counts with a public official or government
facility denominator materially reorder the subnational people-per-facility
rank?

### Main comparison

Philippine ADM1 population divided by OSM clinical points versus the same
population divided by DOH NHFR v2.0 clinical facilities. Bangladesh is a
supporting registry replication. Cambodia is a source-disagreement audit only
because its government inventory is from 2010 and has a narrower public-
provider scope.

### Decision rule and sensitivity

Retire access-ranking language if at least 50% of joined subnational ranks
change. Because 50% is arbitrary, test minus 50% and plus 50% thresholds:
25%, 50%, and 75%. Report all three results and the continuous changed-rank
share.

### Interpretation rule

- If the Philippine result is below 25%, the inherited rank may remain a
  provisional map-based screen with a prominent completeness caveat.
- If it reaches 25% but not 50%, reshape the exact rank and report instability.
- If it reaches 50% or more, retire access-ranking language.
- Regardless of the threshold, do not use travel-time, capacity, utilization,
  quality, affordability, household-access, welfare, or DMC-performance claims.

### Observed decision

Philippines: 16 of 17 ranks changed (94.1%), exceeding 25%, 50%, and 75%.
Bangladesh: 6 of 8 (75.0%). Cambodia: 21 of 24 joined (87.5%), but only as
source disagreement. The public claim is therefore reshaped from a stable
access-stress ranking to a map-observability finding.
