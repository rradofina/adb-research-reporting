# Policy brief

## Finding

Across 1,334 reporting GHS-UCDB urban centres in 24 ADB developing economies,
population below 10 metres increased by **90.9 million** from 2000 to 2020.
Shanghai, Bangkok, and Dhaka lead; the top ten centres account for **52.1%** of
positive change.

## Why it matters

A direct centre-level spatial object changes three of the inherited national
proxy's top five economies. Named-centre evidence is a stronger starting point
for deeper coastal analysis than an imputed country score.

## Robustness

The result remains positive below a stricter 5-metre threshold and across
10-, 20-, and 30-year windows. Population and built-up change correlate at
0.85.

## Use

- Prioritize city-level hazard, subsidence, protection, and service-data joins.
- Report absolute counts alongside low-elevation shares.
- Keep blanks, observed zeros, and absent small-island centres separate.

## Limit

This is a reporting-subset exposure measure—not a flood-loss, informality,
deprivation, adaptation, or performance ranking. `attestation_chain: ai-first`.
