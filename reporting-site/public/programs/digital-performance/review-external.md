## Likely reviewer objections

**“You subtracted unlike indicators.”** Correct. The paper does not claim a
microdata partition. Its object is disagreement between two official monitoring
constructs at the same economy-year. The figures always show both inputs.

**“4G coverage is not usable connectivity.”** Correct. That is the reason for
the paper. Coverage is presented as signal availability, not quality,
reliability, latency, or affordability.

**“2024 values may be estimated.”** Correct. Six headline pairs identify both
components as ITU estimates; other pairs include national sources. The paper
reports provenance, fixes the API vintage by digest, and avoids an accuracy
ranking.

**“The price result is implausibly weak.”** It is plausible because a national
5 GB basket is one partial barrier and the primary difference also reflects
devices, skills, value, trust, geography, and source methods. The paper reports
the weak association rather than forcing an affordability story.

**“The urban–rural result is overfit.”** The paper labels n=10 in the chart and
text, treats the association as hypothesis-generating, and makes expansion of
comparable disaggregated data the next upgrade.

**“Why not use Ookla to validate everything?”** Ookla observes people and
places that generate tests. It can measure performance conditional on testing
and reveal measurement deserts when joined to population. It cannot observe
the unconnected or validate adoption.

## Red-team verdict

The paper is defensible if its title and deck lead with the measurement
difference and all public surfaces retain the non-claims. It would become
misleading if framed as a count of covered non-users, a speed result, or proof
that affordability does not matter.
