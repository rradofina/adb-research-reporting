# Related literature and the evidence gap

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

The logistics literature distinguishes objects that the inherited screen had
combined. The World Bank LPI is a country-level survey of logistics
performance, while the CPPI uses vessel-time evidence at ports
[@wb2023lpi; @worldbank2025cppi]. UNCTAD’s port and maritime indicators add
trade and connectivity context but do not convert national trade scale into a
port-delay measure [@unctad2024rmt].

The measurement implication is direct: import value can describe exposure,
and a perception survey can describe broad conditions, but neither validates a
port-time label. A qualified hinterland study needs an observed route-level
object. World Bank LPI 2.0 identifies shipment indicators for 2023–2024,
including port turnaround and corridor lead time for landlocked economies
[@worldbank2026lpi2]. That file is the named next source, not a justification
for another national proxy.

The contribution is a public-data construct test for ADB developing member
economies. It compares the inherited ordering with observed CPPI diagnostics,
tests year, call-threshold, and aggregation choices, quantifies uncertainty,
and leaves the port-to-inland gate visibly open. The full search record,
inclusion decisions, and marginal-contribution discussion remain in
`literature.md`.
