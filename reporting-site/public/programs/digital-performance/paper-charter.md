# Paper charter — network availability is not internet use

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · 2026-07-19

## Policy problem

Connectivity policy often reports network rollout as progress toward digital
inclusion. A network can be technically available while people remain offline,
or while use is intermittent, unaffordable, low-quality, or constrained by
devices and skills. Infrastructure and use therefore need separate monitoring
objects.

## Research questions

1. How far apart are reported 4G/LTE population coverage and recent internet
   use in exact-year observations across ADB developing member economies?
2. Does the difference narrow between 2018 and 2024 on a balanced sample?
3. Is the difference closely associated with the ITU 5 GB affordability
   basket?
4. Where comparable disaggregations exist, does the national difference move
   with the urban–rural use divide?
5. How much of the apparent result is sensitive to the cross-section coverage
   rule and source provenance?

## Contribution

The paper supplies a reproducible ADB-region crosswalk between two distinct ITU
constructs. Its contribution is not a new connectivity index. It is a
measurement discipline: report availability and use together; preserve exact
years and source notes; expose missingness; and refuse to let a speed-test
sample stand in for adoption.

## Primary claim

In the latest cross-section that meets the prospective sample rule, reported
4G/LTE availability exceeds recent internet use in most observed economies.
The magnitude varies widely and includes negative values that require source
and definition review.

## Claim boundary

The design can describe a difference between official indicators. It cannot
identify a count of “covered but unconnected” individuals because the two
series are not microdata from the same people. It cannot attribute the
difference to affordability, skills, devices, gender, geography, or policy.

## Evidence spine

1. Signed 2024 differences for every observed economy.
2. Both components behind each difference.
3. Coverage-versus-use scatterplot.
4. Annual medians plus annual sample size.
5. Balanced 2018–2024 changes.
6. Affordability association.
7. Urban–rural diagnostic.
8. Source-provenance diagnostic.
9. ±50% sample-floor sensitivity.
10. Exact-year pair availability heatmap.
11. Coverage funnel.
12. Method and claim-gate infographic.
