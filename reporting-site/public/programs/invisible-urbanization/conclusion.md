# Conclusion and next evidence upgrade

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

Urbanization is not a single percentage waiting to be read from a table. Across
40 complete ADB-economy cases in 2020, two legitimate but different statistical
constructs are a median of 20.0 percentage points apart. The disagreement
persists across 1975–2020 and runs in both directions.

The administrative analysis explains a second source of instability. The
share of urban-cell population inside a rural-classified unit rises as the unit
becomes finer. Over time, that stock can fall even while persistent rural units
gain urban-cell residents, because other units cross the standardized
rural-to-town/city threshold.

The practical conclusion is straightforward: show the definition, show the
scale, and show both measures when they diverge. Use WDI for nationally defined
reporting and GHSL for harmonized spatial comparison. Do not infer legal
misclassification or policy neglect from their gap alone.

## Next evidence upgrade

The next study should be a country-specific validation, not a broader global
ranking. Select one high-gap economy and join:

1. dated official census or statutory urban classifications;
2. historical administrative boundary vintages;
3. GHSL built-up and population grids at settlement scale;
4. service, fiscal, or planning outcomes; and
5. qualitative or administrative evidence explaining reclassification rules.

That design could test whether standardized urban growth precedes official
recognition and whether recognition changes resources or services. Until those
objects exist, the defensible finding remains about measurement.
