# One stock, two questions

International migration data often arrive as a league table. Sort economies
by the number of people born there who live abroad, and large origin countries
rise to the top. That is a valid answer to a scale question: where are the
largest diaspora populations from?

It is not an answer to a population-relative question. A country with many
residents can have a large diaspora count even when that count is modest
relative to its resident population. A small island economy can have a much
smaller count and a far larger population-relative stock. Bilateral migrant-
stock research has long stressed that stocks describe accumulated location
patterns, not a single year's movement [@ozden2011where].

The migration and displacement program tested how much this distinction
matters in its 44-economy panel.

# Population enters the denominator

The analysis ranks UN DESA International Migrant Stock 2024 by origin. It then
divides the same stock by World Bank WDI resident population for 2024. The WDI
denominator is available for 41 panel economies. Taipei,China, Cook Islands,
and Niue are withheld from the second ranking because no value is present in
the committed WDI extract.

The absolute top five are India, China, Bangladesh, Afghanistan, and the
Philippines. The population-share top five are Samoa, Tonga, Armenia, Nauru,
and Fiji. **The two sets have zero overlap.**

![Paired rankings show that none of the five largest absolute origin stocks remains in the population-share top five.](/programs/migration-displacement-signals/generated/charts/migration-rank-inversion.svg)

This is not a small adjustment at the margins. India moves from first in
absolute stock to 36th among the 41 economies with a denominator. China moves
from second to 39th. Samoa and Tonga move into the leading positions.

# Small economies carry a different population-relative signal

Samoa's emigrant stock equals 54.73% of its resident population; Tonga's
equals 51.10%. Armenia, Nauru, and Fiji range from 19.49% to 21.02%.

![Population-share bars show Samoa and Tonga above 50%, while India and China remain below 2%.](/programs/migration-displacement-signals/generated/charts/migration-population-share-profile.svg)

The ratio should be named carefully. It compares a cumulative stock abroad
with a resident-population snapshot. It is not the percentage of today's
residents who left in 2024, a migration rate, or a measure of individual
propensity. Migration decisions emerge from aspirations and capabilities as
well as constraints [@dehaas2021aspirations]; this descriptive stock panel
does not identify those mechanisms.

The ranking change is also not driven by a single arbitrary cutoff. The top
three sets have zero overlap. At the top eight, Afghanistan is the only shared
origin—one of eight, or 12.5%. The claim therefore survives top-N choices of
3, 5, and 8 and material-change thresholds of 25%, 50%, and 75%.

# Afghanistan is the construct warning

One origin needs a separate lens. Afghanistan is fourth by absolute stock and
sixth by population-relative stock. In the UNHCR 2024 crosswalk, forced-
displacement stock equals 81.7% of its UN DESA emigrant stock.

That is not true of the population-share top five. Their UNHCR forced-
displacement shares range from 0.4% for Samoa to 5.7% for Armenia. None reaches
25%, while Afghanistan remains above 75%.

![Composition bars separate Afghanistan's forced-displacement-majority profile from the five population-share leaders.](/programs/migration-displacement-signals/generated/charts/migration-forced-displacement-composition.svg)

The residual is deliberately labeled **other or unclassified migrant stock**.
It cannot be renamed labor migration because the joined public sources do not
identify every migration purpose.

# Corridors add context, not a new headline

For the absolute top-five origins, the share going to the three largest
destinations ranges from 45.15% for India to 80.15% for Afghanistan. A 50%
concentration label would include Bangladesh, Afghanistan, and the Philippines
but exclude India and China. That split is threshold-sensitive, so corridor
concentration remains descriptive context.

![Corridor chart shows how the top-two, top-three, and top-five destination shares vary across the absolute-stock leaders.](/programs/migration-displacement-signals/generated/charts/migration-corridor-concentration.svg)

# Better questions follow from clearer measurement

The revised result retires the idea that one origin ranking can stand in for
"migration pressure" or "migration intensity." It supports three narrower
uses:

1. absolute stock for diaspora-scale and service-population questions;
2. stock relative to resident population for population-relative exposure;
3. UNHCR protection categories for identifying forced-displacement cases.

The source audit also shows where the next research object should begin.
UN DESA provides the stock spine, WDI supplies most—but not all—population
denominators, and UNHCR can identify protection categories without classifying
all other migration purposes.

![The observability matrix shows which policy questions the three public sources can and cannot answer.](/programs/migration-displacement-signals/generated/charts/migration-source-observability.svg)

A labor-mobility or current-flow paper would need different public data:
annual inflows and outflows, visa or recruitment records, return movements,
or household evidence with appropriate ethical and disclosure review. Until
then, the strongest finding is the denominator reversal itself—and the need to
keep scale, population-relative stock, and forced displacement separate.
