# The same country can have two very different urban shares

Ask what share of a country is urban and the answer may depend less on the
settlements than on the rule used to classify them.

# Two measures, one median gap of 20.0 points

Across 40 ADB developing-economy cases in 2020, a standardized GHSL measure and
the WDI national-definition series are a median of 20.0 percentage points
apart. In 33 cases GHSL is higher. In seven WDI is higher.

That is not evidence that one source failed. WDI reflects national definitions;
GHSL applies a common grid method. The first is closer to national reporting.
The second is better suited to international comparison.

# Scale and reclassification change the answer again

The administrative unit matters too. In a common 13-economy sample, 0.6% of
urban-cell population sits inside rural-classified units at GADM level 1,
compared with 2.8% at level 3. A finer map exposes a different “hidden” share.

The most revealing result comes from following units over time. From 2000 to
2020, urban-cell population grew by 13.9 million inside units that remained
rural. The total embedded stock still fell because hundreds of units crossed
the standardized rural-to-town/city threshold.

So a declining hidden stock can coexist with continued urban growth. The
classification is moving too.

# Show the definition before making a policy claim

The practical rule is simple: show the definition, show the scale, and show
both measures when they diverge. Then validate law, boundaries, and services
before making a policy claim.
