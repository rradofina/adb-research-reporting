# When urbanization depends on the definition

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · PP measurement study · 2026-07-19

## Issue

Urbanization is routinely presented as a national percentage, but “urban” is
not a universal statistical object. National series reflect country-specific
administrative, population, density, economic, or functional rules. The Global
Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) instead applies a common grid-based Degree of
Urbanisation method. Those measures can disagree even when both are correctly
constructed for their intended use.

This matters because a rural label can govern how analysts describe settlement
change, target surveys, compare service coverage, and frame infrastructure
needs. It does **not** follow that a standardized grid is the legal truth or
that a difference proves neglect. The first research task is therefore to
measure the disagreement and its sensitivity before making a policy claim.

## Research questions

1. How far apart were the GHSL standardized and WDI national-definition urban
   shares across the repository's ADB developing-economy roster in 2020?
2. Is the difference a one-year anomaly or a persistent feature of the
   1975–2020 series?
3. How much GHSL urban-cell population sits inside administrative units that
   GHS-DUC classifies as rural, and does that measure change with unit scale?
4. When the embedded stock falls, is urban growth disappearing, or are units
   crossing the standardized classification threshold?

## Key messages

- Across 40 economies with both measures in 2020, the median absolute gap is
  **20.0 percentage points**. GHSL is higher in 33 cases and WDI in seven.
- The disagreement is persistent: the complete-case median absolute gap stays
  near 20–26 percentage points across every five-year epoch from 1975 to 2020.
- For the same 13 economies, the share of urban-cell population inside
  rural-classified units is **0.6% at administrative level 1, 1.9% at level 2,
  and 2.8% at level 3**. “Hidden” is partly a scale choice.
- Among 7,918 matched level-2 units in 34 economies, units that remained rural
  gained **13.9 million** urban-cell residents from 2000 to 2020. The embedded
  stock nevertheless fell because 678 units crossed from rural to town/city,
  removing 43.3 million from that statistical category.

## Contribution

The paper replaces a rank-preserving WDI proxy with two auditable public-data
objects: a cross-definition panel and an administrative-transition panel. Its
contribution is not a new country ranking. It is a reusable measurement test
showing when urbanization conclusions depend on definition, administrative
scale, and classification dynamics.

## Decision use

Use the national series for nationally defined planning and legal reporting.
Use the harmonized GHSL series for comparable spatial screening. When the two
diverge materially, report both and investigate the country-specific
classification and service context before allocating resources or labelling a
place underserved.
