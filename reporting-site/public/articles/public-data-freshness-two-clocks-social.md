# Primary post

A public-data cell has two clocks.

Calendar age tells you how old the observation is. Relative lag tells you how
far it sits behind the indicator's source frontier.

In a frozen 42-economy × 18-indicator WDI panel, those rules disagree for 138
of 709 observed cells (19.5%) at a three-year cutoff. But the result is
concentrated: environment accounts for 80 disagreements, and removing it
pushes the result below the study's decision gate.

The dashboard lesson is not to hide calendar age. Show latest year, indicator
frontier, relative lag, missingness, and the cutoff—separately.

AI-first Screening Result. Public source objects, scripts, sensitivity tests,
and review records included. No economy or statistical-office ranking.

# Short version

“Three years old” can mean the whole indicator is produced slowly—or one
economy trails the series frontier.

Show both clocks. Keep missingness separate. Make the cutoff visible.
