# The finding

Five rural-majority ADB DMCs hold the top of the invisible-urbanization
signal: Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Afghanistan, Lao PDR,
Bangladesh. The signal is a proxy for sub-classification lag —
settlement growth that may not yet be reflected in WDI's urban
classification.

| ISO3 | DMC | Urban % | Urban-pop growth % | Rural % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PNG | Papua New Guinea | 14 | 4.0 | 86 |
| SLB | Solomon Islands | 26 | 4.2 | 74 |
| AFG | Afghanistan | 27 | 3.8 | 73 |
| LAO | Lao PDR | 38 | 3.2 | 62 |
| BGD | Bangladesh | 41 | 3.2 | 59 |

# What it cannot say

- The signal is a proxy. The canonical "invisible urbanization"
  measure is GHSL Built-up Surface change, not WDI growth-rate
  inference.
- WDI urban definitions vary by country. PNG's "urban" and
  Bangladesh's "urban" are not strictly comparable.
- Sub-classification lag varies with national statistical capacity.
  Some countries update urban classifications often, others rarely.
  The "invisible" reading is co-produced by real growth and
  statistical lag.
