# Paper charter — disaster recovery measurement

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · Last updated 2026-07-18

## Decision audience

Disaster-risk analysts, remote-sensing researchers, recovery-program teams,
and statistical offices deciding whether public night-light data are ready for
a subnational recovery indicator.

## Research question

Can country disaster records, GDIS administrative centroids, and daily public
VIIRS radiance support a stable and interpretable recovery-month measure?

## Contribution

A two-stage construct test: (1) apply the inherited kill rule across five
country burden metrics; (2) run a frozen Typhoon Haiyan recovery pilot across
seven centroids, 108 scheduled orbits, and 54 specifications per centroid.

## Primary claim

The measurement chain fails both gates. Three of five burden metrics replace
the claimed top two, and zero of seven centroids have one recovery month across
all 54 variants.

## Decision rule

Validate only if at least three centroids have four valid baseline months, a
main-spec recovery month, and the identical month under every variant.

## Non-claims

No welfare, resilience, reconstruction, service-restoration, or causal effect;
no economy performance ranking; no claim that nighttime-light data are
generally unusable; no claim that no recovery occurred.

## Stopping rule

The current issue closes when evidence, figures, paper, publication ladder,
public route, review packet, and boards state the same construct-validation
finding. Reopen only for verified event footprints, longer baselines, or an
independent recovery outcome.
