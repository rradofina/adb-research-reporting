# Limitations and non-claims

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

1. **Burden is not recovery.** Event counts, people affected, deaths, and
   reported damage answer different burden questions and cannot identify the
   time required to restore services, livelihoods, or welfare.
2. **Centroids are not footprints.** GDIS centroids represent named
   administrative units. A storm may affect only part of a unit, while a
   centroid window may include untouched or differently affected places.
3. **Radiance is a proxy.** Nighttime light can reflect electricity service,
   cloud and atmospheric effects, settlement brightness, relief activity,
   changing land use, or sensor-processing behavior. It is not household
   welfare, output, reconstruction, or service restoration.
4. **The baseline is thin.** Only one of seven centroids has four valid
   pre-event main-specification months. Ratios around dark baselines can be
   unstable and raw radiance can be negative after background subtraction.
5. **The horizon is limited.** The post-event window ends in October 2014. A
   missing recovery month can mean no detection within the window, not permanent
   non-recovery.
6. **The reference is imperfect.** Manila normalization removes some orbital
   and acquisition variation, but Manila is not an untreated counterfactual.
7. **Geometry audit is coarse.** The 1,000 km country-polygon screen catches
   gross mismatch only. It does not confirm subnational names, boundaries,
   event footprints, or historical administrative consistency.
8. **Burden data are reported data.** EM-DAT thresholds, revisions, missing
   damage, and cross-country reporting differences affect the rank screen
   [@cred2024emdat].
9. **No causal design.** There is no untreated comparison group, event-study
   identification, or adjustment for co-occurring shocks.

The study does not rank economies by resilience, policy quality, or welfare
recovery. It reports where a proposed measurement chain fails.
