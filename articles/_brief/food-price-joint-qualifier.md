# The decision

Retire the annual DMC qualifier and the old Nepal price-wave count. Publish the
corrected result as a construct validation: local dryness is not the common
near-term signature of large coarse-rice price increases in this panel.

![Only 17 of 152 corrected spike cells follow locally dry rainfall](/programs/food-price-climate-transmission/generated/charts/food-price-spike-alignment.svg)

# What changed

The earlier method compared each price with the full-sample median for that
market and calendar month. That mechanically flagged later high price levels.
Using year-on-year log change reduces spike cells from 259 to 152 and broad
wave months from 25 to 10.

# What the corrected evidence shows

The analysis aligns 760 coarse-rice market-month observations with prior-month
rainfall across 12 Nepal markets. Under the main thresholds, 17 of 152 spike
cells are dry-aligned and 135 are not. Across all 81 ±50% threshold
combinations, dry alignment remains below half.

The exact counts of broad waves and dry clusters are unstable, so they are not
headline quantities. Annual headline CPI also fails as a substitute: it has a
five-year rank correlation of +0.21 with median market rice-price change, and
the inherited annual screen does not select Nepal.

# What not to infer

The result does not estimate a climate effect or show that rainfall explains
11.2% of price spikes. Point rainfall at a market is not a recorded production
shock. The panel covers one commodity and lacks market access, fuel, trade,
exchange-rate, production, and policy controls.

# Next evidence object

Build an event-defined, multi-commodity market panel with crop and sourcing
zones, connectivity, common-driver controls, and a fixed-effects, event-study,
or local-projection design. Existing Nepal research already sets that bar
[@baptista2023climateshocks].
