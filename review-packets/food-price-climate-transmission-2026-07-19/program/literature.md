# Literature boundary — Food prices and climate in Nepal

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · 2026-07-19

Baptista, Spray, and Unsal already estimate Nepal district-level food-price
responses to recorded climate shocks using WFP prices, fixed effects, and local
projections [@baptista2023climateshocks]. Their result makes novelty through a
simple rainfall-price coincidence screen unavailable.

Shively and Thapa link Nepal monthly food prices with rainfall, roads, and fuel,
showing why market integration and transport belong in the design
[@shively2017nepalfoodprices]. Kotz et al. estimate climate-inflation responses
from a large monthly CPI panel with fixed effects and trends
[@kotz2024climateinflation]. FAO's FPMA system distinguishes monitoring price
changes from explaining them [@fao2022fpma]. The World Bank's 2025 Nepal update
adds macro, import, and cross-border context [@worldbank2025nepaldevelopmentupdate].

## Research gap that remains

An earned successor would update the Nepal event panel with newer public data,
multiple commodities, geocoded hazards, crop and sourcing zones, market access,
fuel, exchange-rate, trade, and policy controls. It should test whether the
published remoteness gradient persists rather than claim first discovery of
climate-price transmission.
