# Results — Migration and displacement signals

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

## Finding

Which origin economies look largest depends completely on whether migrant
stock is read as an absolute count or relative to the resident population.
The absolute top five—India, China, Bangladesh, Afghanistan, and the
Philippines—has **zero overlap** with the population-share top five—Samoa,
Tonga, Armenia, Nauru, and Fiji.

This is a measurement result, not a country-performance ranking. Absolute
stock describes diaspora scale. Stock divided by population describes the
size of that cumulative stock relative to the resident population. Neither
measure is a current migration flow.

*Figure 1. Rank positions for the union of the two top-five sets. Source: UN
DESA International Migrant Stock 2024 and WDI 2024 population. Taipei,China,

## Population normalization changes the policy object

India has 18.5 million people in the origin stock and ranks first in absolute
size, but that stock equals 1.28% of its 2024 resident population and places
36th among the 41 economies with denominators. China moves from second to
39th. Bangladesh moves from third to 25th, and the Philippines from fifth to
23rd.

The population-share leaders are different. Samoa's 119,313 people abroad
equal 54.73% of its resident population, and Tonga's 53,237 equal 51.10%.
Armenia, Nauru, and Fiji complete the top five at 21.02%, 20.79%, and 19.49%.

![Top twelve emigrant-stock shares of resident population](generated/charts/migration-population-share-profile.png)

*Figure 2. UN DESA origin stock divided by WDI mid-year population, 2024.
The bars do not represent people who departed in 2024. Older diaspora waves,
family mobility, labor schemes, study, and forced movement can all contribute
to the numerator.*

## The denominator result survives the sensitivity suite

At top three, the absolute and population-share sets still have zero overlap.
At top eight, only Afghanistan appears in both sets, an overlap of 1/8 or
12.5%. The denominator-sensitive conclusion therefore passes the 25%, 50%,
and 75% material-change thresholds for every tested N.

The result does not establish that the share ordering is uniquely correct.
It establishes that the old absolute ordering cannot be presented as
migration intensity.

## Corridor concentration is secondary and threshold-sensitive

The five largest absolute origin stocks place 45% to 80% of their stock in
their top three destinations. Bangladesh, Afghanistan, and the Philippines
clear the inherited 50% threshold; India and China do not. But all five clear
25%, while only Afghanistan clears 75%. Changing the number of destinations
from two to five also changes the apparent level substantially.

![Destination concentration sensitivity for the five largest absolute stocks](generated/charts/migration-corridor-concentration.png)

*Figure 3. Share of each origin's UN DESA emigrant stock in its top two, three,
or five destination economies. The 50% reference is heuristic. The corridors
combine migration purposes and historical periods.*

The concentration split is therefore retained as descriptive context, not as
the headline. It says where cumulative stock is recorded, not why people
moved or whether a corridor is currently active.

## Afghanistan is the construct exception

Afghanistan moves from absolute rank 4 to population-share rank 6, making it
the only large absolute origin close to the population-share leaders. The
UNHCR crosswalk changes the interpretation: 6,151,318 people in the selected
international forced-displacement categories equal 81.7% of Afghanistan's
UN DESA emigrant stock.

None of the population-share top five is forced-displacement-majority. Their
UNHCR forced-displacement components range from 0.4% for Samoa to 5.7% for
Armenia. That does not make the remainder labor migration; it remains other or
unclassified migrant stock.

![UNHCR forced-displacement share for Afghanistan and the population-share top five](generated/charts/migration-forced-displacement-composition.png)

*Figure 4. Refugees, asylum-seekers, and other people in need of international
protection abroad as a share of UN DESA origin stock, 2024. IDPs are excluded
from the numerator because they remain inside the origin economy. The residual
cannot be classified by purpose with these sources.*

Afghanistan remains above the forced-displacement threshold at 25%, 50%, and
75%. Every population-share top-five economy remains below 25%. The construct
distinction is therefore insensitive to the required ±50% threshold test.

## Source coverage bounds the result

All 44 program economies have a UN DESA stock row. WDI provides the 2024
population denominator for 41. The UNHCR script queried all 44 origins and
found at least one positive forced-abroad row for 41. These counts refer to
different source functions and must not be treated as interchangeable
coverage rates.

![Coverage of the three joined migration evidence layers](generated/charts/migration-source-observability.png)

*Figure 5. Source observability in the committed program panel. Missing WDI
denominators are explicit. A missing positive UNHCR forced-abroad row is not
proof of zero displacement.*

## Interpretation

For operational use, the two denominators answer different questions:

- absolute stock identifies large diaspora systems where bilateral links may
  matter at scale;
- population share identifies economies where cumulative stock abroad is
  large relative to the resident population; and
- the UNHCR crosswalk identifies when the stock is substantially a protection
  caseload rather than an undifferentiated diaspora measure.

The evidence supports a source-selection decision. It does not support a
claim about current migration pressure, the welfare consequences of mobility,
or the causes of movement.

## Conclusion

The old absolute-stock headline is retired. A denominator must be named before
an origin ranking is interpreted, and a migration-purpose source is needed
before the same stock is described as labor, family, student, or forced
mobility. The current data are sufficient for that measurement correction and
no further stock-ranking pass is needed in this issue.
