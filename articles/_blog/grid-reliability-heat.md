# Two plausible risks, one incomplete public panel

Heat can stress electricity systems. Concentrated generation can expose a
system to fuel- or weather-specific shocks. Both ideas are plausible. But the
public regional data do not turn them into one stable ranking.

# Fuel concentration survives a harder check

The generation-mix result survives a better test: the same five economies
remain most concentrated when installed capacity is replaced by 2017
generation, and most systems look more concentrated in what they actually
generate.

# Heat and reliability do not lock into one direction

The heat result fails. Joining three World Bank CCKP heat measures to five
public reliability proxies in the same country and year produces eight
positive and seven negative correlations. Most uncertainty intervals include
zero. Even the same outage outcome changes sign when heat changes definition.

# What the negative result is good for

That is a useful result. It stops a plausible mechanism from becoming an
overconfident regional leaderboard. The next research design needs real
interruption timing and grid operating conditions—not another national proxy.
