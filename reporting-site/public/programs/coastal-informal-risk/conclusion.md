# Conclusion — from a proxy ranking to a city research queue

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

The inherited national score could generate a ranking, but not a coastal
finding. Replacing it with a direct spatial object changes three things. The
unit becomes a named urban centre, the measured quantity becomes population
and built-up surface inside a low-elevation mask, and the limits become visible.

The reporting subset added 90.9 million people below 10 metres from 2000 to
2020. The increase is concentrated: Shanghai, Bangkok, and Dhaka lead, and ten
centres contribute just over half of all positive change. The result remains
positive under a stricter 5-metre definition and ±50% time-window choices.

This does not finish coastal-risk research. It produces a defensible queue for
the next stage. The next study should take one or a small number of centres and
join relative sea level, storm surge, subsidence, protection, local boundaries,
and validated deprivation or service data. That is where exposure can become
risk and where a planning claim can be tested.
