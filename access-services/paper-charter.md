# Paper charter — When the map changes the rank

`attestation_chain: ai-first`. Claim reshape recorded 2026-07-18.

## Decision problem

A planner can use open map data to find places where the facility layer needs
urgent validation. The same data should not be presented as a comparative
service-access ranking when uneven mapping can determine the denominator.

## Research question

When population is divided by OSM-tagged health-facility points, how much of
the resulting subnational rank is associated with map completeness rather than
the official clinical-facility denominator?

## Headline claim

Replacing OSM health-point counts with the official clinical registry reorders
16 of 17 Philippine regional people-per-facility ranks. Across those regions,
OSM capture and apparent facility load are strongly negatively associated
(Spearman rho = -0.81). The inherited eight-economy screen is therefore a
map-observability triage, not a service-access ranking.

## Supporting checks

- Bangladesh registry counts reorder 6 of 8 division ranks.
- Only the Philippines and Bangladesh have comparable registry joins across
  the eight-economy pilot.
- Cambodia's 2010 public-facility inventory reorders 21 of 24 joined province
  ranks, but the 16-year source-vintage and provider-scope mismatch makes that
  a source-disagreement result, not a current completeness estimate.

## Non-claims

The study does not measure travel time, transport mode, facility capacity,
service availability, quality, utilization, affordability, household access,
or welfare. It does not rank DMC performance.

## Publication decision

Retire the old stable-top-four access headline. Publish the denominator
falsification as the finding, retain the eight-economy screen only as a source
validation queue, and keep maturity at PP.
