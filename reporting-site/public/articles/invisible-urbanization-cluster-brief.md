# Policy brief

## Finding

Across 40 ADB developing-economy cases with both measures in 2020, the median
absolute gap between the GHSL standardized urban share and WDI's
national-definition share is **20.0 percentage points**. GHSL is higher in 33
cases and WDI in seven.

## Why it matters

Urban percentages can guide comparisons, surveys, and infrastructure framing,
but national definitions are not uniform. A harmonized grid improves
comparability without replacing national legal definitions.

## Second result

For the same 13 economies, urban-cell population inside rural-classified units
rises from **0.6% at GADM level 1 to 2.8% at level 3**. Administrative scale is
part of the measurement.

From 2000 to 2020, persistently rural level-2 units gained 13.9 million
urban-cell residents even as the total embedded stock fell. Units crossing the
standardized threshold explain the difference.

## Use

- Report the definition behind the urban share.
- Show national and harmonized measures together when they diverge.
- Validate legal classification and service conditions before targeting.

## Limit

The study does not prove legal misclassification, policy neglect, or welfare
effects. `attestation_chain: ai-first`.
