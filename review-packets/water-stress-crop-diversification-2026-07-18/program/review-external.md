## Objections and responses

### C-1 — available renewable water is not usable water

SDG 6.4.2 improves the denominator by accounting for renewable resources and
environmental-flow requirements, but AQUASTAT cautions that total renewable
flows are not fully exploitable. A national percentage should not be described
as the water available to farms.

**Response.** Accepted. The article calls SDG 6.4.2 a direct national
monitoring object and does not interpret it as basin supply or farm access.

### C-2 — water risk is multidimensional and spatial

Aqueduct separates baseline stress, depletion, drought, variability, quality,
and governance. A single national stress value cannot replace those dimensions.

**Response.** Accepted. The ranking is retired. The next object requires basin
withdrawal or depletion and allocation rather than another national score.

### C-3 — annual national means hide seasonality

Monthly scarcity can be severe even when an annual national ratio appears
moderate. The validation may miss seasonal crop-water coincidence.

**Response.** Accepted. No seasonal or crop-calendar claim is made; common-year
weather and crop water requirements are named as required layers.

### C-4 — concentration is not resilience

Crop diversity can be associated with production stability, but HHI alone does
not measure response to drought or heat. The study needs a shock outcome.

**Response.** Accepted. The paper treats HHI as a concentration descriptor and
specifies production loss, yield variance, depletion, or recovery as possible
future outcomes.

### C-5 — Central Asian ratios need transboundary allocation

Downstream withdrawals and upstream control are linked. National ratios can
misattribute the location and governance mechanism of pressure.

**Response.** Accepted. The article uses Central Asia to motivate basin
allocation and avoids attributing the ratio to domestic depletion alone.
