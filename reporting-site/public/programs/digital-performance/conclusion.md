# Conclusion and next statistical upgrade

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · 2026-07-19

Reported network rollout and actual internet use do not tell the same story.
In 2024, the median availability–use difference is 14.3 percentage points
across 34 exact-year ADB developing member cases, and 31 differences are
positive. The result survives the prospective ±50% sample-floor check.

The research implication is practical: connectivity dashboards should place
availability and use side by side, retain the year and source method, and show
the missing denominator. A national coverage percentage should not be used as
a proxy for adoption, and a speed-test median should not be used as a proxy for
the unconnected.

The evidence also narrows what should happen next.

1. Expand comparable urban/rural and demographic internet-use series; the
   current exact-year regional sample is too small.
2. Join household or distribution-sensitive affordability measures, device
   access, digital skills, and stated barriers to non-use.
3. Audit negative and extreme differences against national metadata and
   revised source vintages.
4. Add Ookla only as a distinct quality layer—performance conditional on a
   test—with measurement deserts reported alongside speed.

The stopping rule is satisfied. The original heavy Ookla pull is no longer a
prerequisite for a defensible paper. It is a future source upgrade with a
different estimand.
