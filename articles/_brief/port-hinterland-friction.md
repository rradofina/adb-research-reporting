# What we found

An inherited screen multiplied import scale by a national logistics-performance gap and labeled the result port-hinterland friction. When tested against the World Bank Container Port Performance Index (CPPI), only Indonesia remains in the inherited top five [@worldbank2025cppi].

The main 2025 test covers 65 ports in 13 matched ADB developing member economies, retaining ports with at least 48 sampled calls. China moves from inherited rank 1 to observed-disadvantage rank 12; India moves from 2 to 10. Across 20 year, call-threshold, and aggregation choices, top-five overlap stays between zero and two.

![Rank inversion between the inherited screen and the observed CPPI diagnostic.](/programs/port-hinterland-friction/generated/charts/port-rank-inversion.svg)

Import value measures trade exposure. LPI measures broad perceived logistics conditions. CPPI measures vessel time at ports. A stable formula using the first two does not become a port-delay measure simply because its ranking is reproducible.

The call-weighted association with observed disadvantage is −0.57; its 95% bootstrap interval is −0.83 to −0.11. The clearest relationship points opposite the inherited label.

# What this means

Retire the imports × LPI ranking as a measure of port or hinterland friction. Use CPPI to diagnose the port boundary and preserve port-level heterogeneity. Do not convert the country diagnostics into a new official ranking.

# What this does not say

CPPI stops at the port boundary. It does not observe inland destination, corridor time, reliability, customs release, cost, or network impedance. World Bank LPI 2.0 identifies an observed shipment layer for 2023–2024 [@worldbank2026lpi2], but the interactive file is behind an access challenge in this environment. The honest result therefore closes the port-time construct test and leaves the hinterland gate open.
