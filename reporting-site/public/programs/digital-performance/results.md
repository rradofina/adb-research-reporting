# Results

`attestation_chain: ai-first` · 2026-07-19

## The 2024 difference is widespread

The prospective rule selects 2024: it is the latest year through 2024 with
exact-year pairs for at least 22 of the 44 roster economies. The cross-section
contains 34 economies. Reported 4G/LTE coverage exceeds internet use in 31;
the median difference is **14.3 percentage points**. Median coverage is 98.0%
and median use is 82.2%.

![Signed 2024 availability–use differences for all 34 exact-year cases. Negative values are retained.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-01-availability-use-gap-hero.svg)

The largest positive differences are Papua New Guinea (57.2 points), Bangladesh
(46.2), Micronesia (45.5), Sri Lanka (42.8), and Nepal (39.7). Kiribati
(-27.4), Nauru (-4.0), and Kazakhstan (-1.8) are negative. Those values do not
prove over- or under-reporting; they flag differences in source, population,
definition, or estimation that deserve review.

## High coverage is not the same as high use

Several large differences occur where reported 4G coverage is near universal.
Bangladesh reports 99.6% coverage and 53.4% use; Sri Lanka reports 97.4% and
54.6%; India reports 99.1% and 64.9%; and the Philippines reports 98.7% and
67.3%.

![Both source components behind each 2024 difference.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-02-components-dumbbell.svg)

![Coverage against use with an equality line.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-03-coverage-use-scatter.svg)

This pattern changes the policy question. In those cases, additional rollout
may still matter locally, but national network presence alone cannot account
for the observed use rate.

## The gap narrowed unevenly

Annual cross-sectional medians show 4G coverage rising faster than use during
the earlier part of the series, but the annual comparison membership changes.
The denominator is therefore plotted with the trend.

![Cross-sectional medians and annual exact-year sample sizes.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-04-components-over-time.svg)

The balanced 2018–2024 comparison contains 28 economies. Its median gap change
is -5.9 points: the gap narrows in 16 economies and widens in 12. Progress is
not uniform, and a regional median should not be read as every economy moving
in the same direction.

![Balanced 2018–2024 change in the availability–use gap.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-05-balanced-gap-change.svg)

## The price basket is not a sufficient explanation

Thirty-two headline economies also have a 2024 ITU 5 GB basket value. The
Spearman association between basket cost as a share of GNI per capita and the
availability–use difference is only 0.16 (Pearson 0.20). High-cost cases such
as Papua New Guinea and Micronesia have large gaps, but Bangladesh and Sri
Lanka have large gaps alongside baskets below 1% of GNI per capita.

![The 2024 5 GB basket and availability–use gap.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-06-affordability-association.svg)

This does not show that affordability is unimportant. It shows that one
national price basket cannot explain the cross-economy pattern by itself.

## A second divide appears inside the small matched sample

Only ten headline economies also have exact-year urban and rural internet-use
rates. Within that restricted sample, the national availability–use difference
and the urban-minus-rural use gap have a Spearman association of 0.93. The
relationship is visually strong but not generalizable to the 44-economy roster.

![National and urban-rural use gaps for ten exact-year cases.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-07-urban-rural-use-gap.svg)

The small result supports a next-data priority: expand comparable rural and
urban survey reporting before interpreting a national availability–use gap as
spatially uniform.

## Provenance and missingness remain part of the result

The median difference is 15.8 points among the six pairs where both component
source strings identify ITU estimates and 14.3 points among the 28 mixed or
national-source pairs. The sign of the median is not driven by one provenance
group, but the source mix limits accuracy comparisons.

![2024 gap distributions by source provenance.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-08-source-provenance.svg)

## Interpretation

The evidence supports one measured conclusion: reported network availability
and recent internet use are materially different monitoring objects across the
observed DMC cases. It supports follow-up on adoption barriers and subnational
inequality. It does not identify which intervention—coverage, price, devices,
skills, content, trust, or regulation—should be prioritized without additional
evidence.
