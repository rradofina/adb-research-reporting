# Results — two validity gates fail

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

## 1. The country burden claim fails its own metric test

The claimed China–India top two survives total affected and adjusted damage.
It does not survive events per year, total deaths, or events per million. The
five top-two sets are CHN–IDN, CHN–IND, CHN–IND, IDN–MMR, and TUV–MHL,
respectively. Three of five metrics therefore activate the recorded kill rule.

This disagreement is substantive. Absolute totals emphasize populous and
asset-rich economies, event frequency emphasizes reporting and hazard incidence,
deaths emphasize rare severe events, and the indicative per-million screen
elevates small island economies. None of these is a recovery-time measure.

## 2. The source bridge is accessible

GDIS contains 609 unique disaster IDs, 565 unique disaster numbers, and 2,881
location rows for ADB developing member economies during the 2012–2018 overlap
with VIIRS nighttime lights. The World Bank Light Every Night archive can be
read without credentials. The Haiyan pilot completed all 108 scheduled-orbit
requests and retained missing or invalid pairings explicitly.

## 3. The recovery construct is not stable

No one of the seven centroids returns the same recovery month across all 54
variants. In the main specification, Aklan returns in March 2014, Palawan in
September, and Samar in June; Capiz, Cebu, Iloilo, and Leyte do not meet the
90%-for-two-month rule within the observation window. Yet only Samar has four
valid pre-event baseline months, and every centroid changes outcome under at
least one reasonable specification.

The number of distinct outcomes per centroid ranges from three to seven,
including no detected recovery. No-detection shares range from 12 of 54
variants for Aklan and Samar to 44 of 54 for Cebu. These are measurement
instability results, not rankings of local recovery performance.

## 4. Geometry requires validation before scaling

A deliberately conservative country-polygon screen flags 3 of 2,881 candidate
centroids more than 1,000 km from the associated country: Fiji Eastern, Fiji
Northern, and the Philippine Cordillera entry. The test catches gross errors;
it does not validate impact geography. GDIS itself describes its polygons and
centroids as administrative approximations rather than true impact zones
[@rosvold2021gdis].

## Interpretation

The public infrastructure passes an access test and fails a measurement test.
That distinction matters. A scalable archive does not automatically yield a
credible outcome. The next useful object is an event-footprint validation set
with a longer local baseline and a comparison against service-restoration or
household outcomes. Until then, this package supports a construct-validation
finding only.
