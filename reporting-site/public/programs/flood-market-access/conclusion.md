# Conclusion and next evidence upgrade

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

The Sylhet pilot replaces the retired country-level flood-rural composite with
the object the research question actually requires: observed flood water,
historical roads, mapped marketplaces, and gridded population joined on one
footprint. Under the base mechanical stress test, 345,718 people—41.24% of the
baseline-accessible covered population—lose a route to eight mapped
marketplaces. The direction is stable across all 54 required variants, whose
disconnected shares range from 38.92% to 43.45%.

That stability validates the routed construct, not the condition of the road
network during the event. Every road edge intersecting mapped flood water is
treated as unavailable; no observed passability, bridge failure, flood depth,
traffic, boat travel, market opening status, or household destination choice is
joined. The result therefore supports a bounded claim about modeled route
disconnection inside the UNOSAT analysis footprint. It does not support road
investment targeting, welfare loss, food-security effects, or a national
estimate.

The next evidence upgrade is deliberately narrow: validate road and bridge
passability and audit the eight mapped market destinations inside the same
footprint. Only after those two gates pass should the factory add observed
travel or market outcomes and test whether the modeled disconnections identify
material access loss.

All empirical values trace to
`flood-market-access/scripts/build-sylhet-route-pilot.py`, its generated JSON
and CSV artifacts, and the public-source custody records described in
`flood-market-access/REPRODUCE.md`.
