# The bars on a phone are not the same as being online

Imagine a mobile network reaches your village. The map turns from grey to
covered. But the handset is too expensive, the data plan competes with food and
transport, the signal fades indoors, or there is no compelling local content.
The infrastructure statistic improved. Your use may not have changed.

That distinction appears clearly in the data. Across 34 ADB developing member
economies with exact-year ITU observations in 2024, reported 4G/LTE coverage
exceeds internet use by a median **14.3 percentage points**.

![The 2024 difference between reported 4G/LTE coverage and internet use.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-01-availability-use-gap-hero.svg)

Bangladesh reports 99.6% coverage and 53.4% use. The Philippines reports 98.7%
and 67.3%. These are not estimates of people who are definitely covered but
offline—the two indicators come from different aggregate measurement systems.
They are signals that rollout and adoption answer different questions.

# Why we did not start with the biggest file

The old research plan began with gigabytes of Ookla Speedtest tiles. Those data
are valuable for a different question: what performance did connected users
who ran a test experience? They cannot see the people who never connect or
never test.

So the research worked backward. First check whether official availability and
actual use line up. Then decide whether speed earns a place in the story.

![Both components behind the difference.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-02-components-dumbbell.svg)

# Price matters, but one basket is not the answer

The ITU 5 GB price basket has only a weak relationship with the 2024
availability–use difference across 32 matched cases. That does not make
affordability irrelevant. It means a national basket cannot stand in for
household income, device cost, digital skills, trust, or the value people see
in going online.

![The national 5 GB price basket does not explain the cross-economy pattern by itself.](/programs/digital-performance/generated/charts/digital-performance-06-affordability-association.svg)

The more revealing secondary signal is also the least complete: in the ten
economies with same-year urban and rural use rates, the urban–rural divide
moves closely with the national gap. Ten is not enough for a regional verdict.
It is enough to tell us what data should be improved next.

# Better dashboards can start now

Connectivity monitoring does not need another opaque index. It needs a better
sequence:

- network availability;
- actual use;
- quality conditional on use;
- affordability and devices;
- skills, trust, content, and outcomes.

Each layer should keep its own denominator. That is less dramatic than one
score, but much more useful for deciding what problem remains.
