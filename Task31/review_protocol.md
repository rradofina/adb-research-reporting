# Task31 review protocol

## Working title

**Welfare Losses from Aggregate Socioeconomic and Environmental Shocks in Asia and the Pacific: A Multidimensional Evidence Review**

## Review design and cutoff

This is a structured rapid evidence review with systematic-scoping elements. Searches cover material available through **31 July 2026**. The review prioritizes causal or quantitatively transparent peer-reviewed evidence, then high-quality institutional estimates where journal evidence is unavailable or too old to represent recent shocks. It is designed as a flagship-report chapter and evidence annex, not as a pooled meta-analysis.

## Geographic scope

The primary population is the developing member countries of the Asian Development Bank (ADB), grouped into East Asia, Southeast Asia, South Asia, Central and West Asia, and the Pacific. Developed Asia-Pacific economies are retained when they provide important comparative, methodological, or regional evidence. Global studies are eligible only when they report Asia-Pacific estimates, contain a large Asia-Pacific sample, or establish a method used to value welfare losses in the region.

## Shock scope

1. **COVID-19 (2020 onward):** mortality and morbidity; household income and consumption; employment and earnings; poverty and inequality; education and lifetime earnings; nutrition; mental health; gender and migration effects.
2. **Economic shocks (shock year 2015 onward):** inflation and cost-of-living crises; food and energy price shocks; supply-chain and trade disruptions; recessions and slowdowns; demonetization and liquidity shocks; financial, debt, and balance-of-payments crises; and geopolitical shocks. Pandemic-mediated outcomes are assigned to COVID-19 unless a study separately identifies another exposure.
3. **Environmental shocks:** tropical cyclones, floods, droughts, heatwaves, wildfire and haze, earthquakes and compound disasters where relevant to regional comparisons, environmental degradation (especially air pollution), and observed or projected climate change. Observed disaster impacts, climate-attributable impacts, and scenario projections are reported separately.

## Welfare framework

Welfare is multidimensional and follows capability, human-development, and welfare-economics perspectives. Outcomes are classified as:

- economic resources: income, consumption, poverty, employment, earnings, assets, wealth, and GDP-equivalent or consumption-equivalent welfare;
- human capital: learning, schooling, skills, nutrition, physical development, lifetime earnings, and intergenerational effects;
- health and subjective wellbeing: mortality, excess mortality, morbidity, DALYs, life expectancy, mental health, and quality of life;
- distribution and social resilience: inequality, gender gaps, informality, migration, exclusion, coping, asset depletion, care burdens, and social-protection adequacy;
- persistence: transitory (up to one year), medium run (one to five years), or long run/intergenerational (more than five years).

Children are ages 0-17, working-age adults 18-64, and older persons 65 or older. A study using different age bands is retained with its original population definition and mapped cautiously to these groups.

## Eligibility

Included studies must provide a quantitative welfare estimate or a transparent model linking an aggregate shock to a welfare outcome. Eligible designs include experiments and quasi-experiments; panel and repeated-cross-section studies; microsimulation and general-equilibrium models; excess-mortality and burden-of-disease estimation; disaster needs assessments with transparent accounting; and climate-impact models with explicit scenarios and uncertainty.

Purely descriptive commentary, untraceable secondary claims, advocacy estimates without methods, and studies reporting only hazard exposure without a welfare consequence are excluded from headline conclusions. Exposure-only studies may be used to characterize vulnerability and geographic reach, clearly labeled as such.

## Search strategy

Search channels include OpenAlex, Crossref, PubMed and journal sites; ADB, World Bank, IMF, UNDP, ESCAP, ILO, WHO, UNICEF, UNESCO, and OECD repositories; backward and forward citation chaining from anchor studies; and targeted searches of the journals named in the commissioning brief. Search concepts combine geographic terms (Asia, Pacific, subregions, and country names), shocks, welfare outcomes, demographic groups, and causal or valuation terminology.

The search log records query, channel, date, result or source selected, and screening decision. Where subscription access is unavailable, metadata and estimates are verified against open full text, accepted manuscripts, official working-paper versions, or institutional source tables; such substitutions are flagged.

## Extraction fields

Each study record contains citation; year; publication type; DOI or stable URL; geography and subregion; ADB-DMC status; population and demographic group; shock and period; welfare domain and outcome; unit and price basis; identification strategy or model; counterfactual; point estimate and uncertainty; persistence; heterogeneity; limitations; page/table locator when available; and a confidence assessment.

## Comparability and double-counting rules

Original units are preserved. Monetary estimates are not converted across price years or PPP concepts unless the source reports the required deflator and basis. GDP losses, asset damages, household income losses, willingness-to-pay valuations, and consumption-equivalent welfare are not treated as interchangeable. Regional aggregates are not added to constituent-country estimates. Overlapping estimates from the same underlying dataset or model are treated as one evidence family.

COVID-related labor, poverty, education, and health effects remain in the COVID category. A later energy, food, debt, or geopolitical shock is placed in the economic category only when the study identifies that exposure separately. Environmental damages are distinguished from the fraction formally attributed to anthropogenic climate change.

## Confidence rubric

- **High:** credible design or transparent burden model; direct welfare outcome; adequate uncertainty; appropriate counterfactual; and corroboration across studies, countries, or methods.
- **Medium:** credible but model-dependent estimate, narrower geography, weaker external validity, incomplete uncertainty, or limited corroboration.
- **Low:** descriptive attribution, strong scenario dependence, weak counterfactual, incomplete methods, exposure used as a proxy for welfare, or a single untriangulated estimate.

Confidence applies to synthesized findings, not journal prestige. Identification, measurement, precision, representativeness, reproducibility, consistency, and directness are considered separately.

## Planned synthesis

The chapter uses narrative synthesis and structured quantitative comparisons. Figure 2 uses separate scales or normalized evidence bands rather than forcing deaths, poverty, learning, and GDP into a single cardinal ranking. The comparative-shock table ranks short-run reach and severity, long-run persistence, evidence strength, and confidence, with explicit reasons. All headline estimates must appear in the extraction workbook and complete reference list.

## Publication caveat

This rapid review can support an advanced flagship-report draft. Before journal submission or formal designation as a systematic review, the search should be refreshed through ADB Library access to Scopus, Web of Science, EconLit, and subscription full text, followed by independent dual screening and duplicate extraction of headline estimates.
