# Limitations and nonclaims

`attestation_chain: ai-first`

1. **Different constructs and denominators.** WDI and GHSL are not two noisy
   readings of one latent truth. They use different definitions and population
   estimation systems. Their percentage-point gap cannot be converted into a
   number of “missing urban people.”
2. **No legal classification ledger.** GHS-DUC assigns a standardized class to
   GADM units. It does not record a government's statutory, census, municipal,
   or fiscal designation. A GHS-DUC transition is not a legal redesignation.
3. **Administrative levels are not comparable institutions.** GADM level 2 may
   represent districts, counties, provinces, or other units of very different
   size and authority. Country ordering is descriptive only.
4. **Modeled population.** GHSL population is spatially modeled. The allocation
   of people within cells and boundaries can affect urban shares.
5. **Official threshold set only.** The analysis does not rerun DEGURBA under
   alternative density, size, contiguity, smoothing, or population-grid rules.
6. **Fixed GADM 4.1 geography.** Historical epochs are applied to current fixed
   boundaries. This supports consistent comparison but does not reproduce the
   administrative map in force at each historical date.
7. **Coverage narrows with depth.** Forty economies enter the definition gap,
   34 the level-2 analysis, and 13 the levels 1–3 comparison.
8. **No service or welfare outcomes.** The study does not measure water,
   transport, schools, health access, budgets, land tenure, housing, poverty,
   productivity, or climate exposure.
9. **No lived-identity validation.** Residents may understand their settlement
   as urban, rural, peri-urban, or something else; the data do not observe it.
10. **No causal claim.** The analysis is descriptive measurement research. It
    does not estimate the effect of reclassification on investment or welfare.

The correct use is as a diagnostic: when national and standardized measures
diverge, investigate the legal classification, boundary history, functions,
and service conditions before making a policy judgment.
